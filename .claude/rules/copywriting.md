# Copywriting Rules

Applies to all user-facing text: UI strings, locale files, marketing copy, store
listings, meta descriptions, and empty states.

## No contrastive-negation framing ("It's not X, it's Y")

Never define something by pairing a negation with an affirmation for rhetorical
effect. State what the thing IS and let the affirmative claim carry the
sentence. Banned variants:

- "It's not X, it's Y." / "It isn't X – it's Y."
- "X, not Y." (e.g., "live and interactive, not screenshots")
- "not just X" / "more than just X" / "more than a Y"
- "X, never Y."

Test: if the sentence still carries its full meaning after deleting the negative
half, delete the negative half.

Still allowed (informative negation, not rhetorical contrast):

- Factual absence where the absence IS the feature: "no email needed".
- Subordinated comparisons that carry real information: "tap-again confirmations
  instead of modal dialogs", "labels wrap instead of overflowing".
- Factual or legal statements: privacy claims, status, error text.

## Dashes

- En dashes (`–`) only, never em dashes (`—` or `&mdash;`).
- Never a double hyphen (`--`) as a sentence dash in user-facing text.
- Never a bare hyphen (`-`) as a sentence dash. Use an en dash.
